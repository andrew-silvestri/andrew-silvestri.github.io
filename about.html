Gas measurement, process simulation (ProMax), and the data tooling between them.

Most numbers in public argument are estimates that travel as if they were measurements. Lead in gasoline is a clean case. The lead in American children's blood was measured, by a national survey, from 1976 on; the IQ points it cost them come from a model. A 2022 estimate by McFarland, Hauer and Reuben puts the loss at 824 million points among Americans alive in 2015, and other published dose-response curves, applied to the same blood lead, give 370 million to 1.95 billion. The choice of curve alone moves the answer by a factor of five. The measurement is firm; the loss is only as firm as the curve.

This site rebuilds numbers like that one from their public sources and shows where they bend. Most pages give the finding, how it is known, and the choice that moves it most, and the code that produced it is a download on the Code page, so you can change the choice and run it again. Everything here comes from public data, public standards and published correlations, each cited on its page. Work done for an employer stays with the employer.

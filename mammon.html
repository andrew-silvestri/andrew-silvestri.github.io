The Mammon trilogy

Three books, one method: read modern civilisation as an energy system and see what the thermodynamics says about greed, growth and maintenance. In progress, deliberately slowly.

Everything else on this site is a model that either runs or does not. This is the exception: a long-form argument being built at the rate long-form arguments are actually built, one session a week over five years. The page exists so the structure and the sources are stated in public early, which is a different and more useful thing than announcing a finished book.

Work in progress

Started April 2026 · target 2030–31

This page describes a project that is being written, not a finished one. It exists to state the structure and the sources honestly at an early stage. Book I is in its first year: reading and monthly essays, no chapter drafts yet. Books II and III exist as sketches that get updated when work on Book I changes what they need to be.

The three books

I · Mammon — the thermodynamics of greed

The first and the one under construction. The argument to be tested is that accumulation behaves like a physical process operating under real constraints rather than a moral failing that happens to have economic consequences — and that if that framing holds, greed should show system-level signatures that are measurable rather than merely deplorable. The intended form is diagnostic and case-driven rather than polemical.

II · Moloch — the potential energy lie

The middle book, in sketch. Its subject is the promise embedded in stored potential: what a civilisation believes it can spend, against what a stock will actually release and at what rate. Debt as borrowed future energy sits here, which is why Graeber is on the reading list for the year before this book begins in earnest.

III · Acedia — the thermodynamics of maintenance

The last, and the one the other two are pointed at. Building is thermodynamically cheap next to keeping. Acedia takes the position that the characteristic failure of a complex society is not an inability to build but an unwillingness to maintain, and that this has an energetic description rather than only a psychological one. Schrödinger’s entropy-defiance framing and Prigogine’s dissipative structures are the physics this book has to earn.

What is being read, and why

Six books, sequenced by when the argument needs them, plus responsive reading — a chapter pulled when a specific problem demands it, rather than another book read cover to cover. Notes are written as synthesis in the reader’s own sentences, not extracted quotes; one file per book.

How it is being written

One session a week, each producing written output and a log entry naming the next action, so re-entry costs nothing. Year one is voice and foundation: one essay a month, two books synthesised. Year two converts the strongest essays into chapter drafts and fixes the outline. Year three drafts all of Mammon. Year four rewrites it from scratch using that draft as source.

One rule governs the manuscript: no AI generates, edits or revises any sentence that appears in it. Pressure-testing an argument, finding what a concept is already called in the literature, and bibliography logistics are permitted; prose is not. The voice that develops over years of sessions is the book, and there is no shortcut to it.

That rule applies to this page too, in the following sense: what you have read here is a description of the project written from its own repository documents — the workflow, the reading schedule, the session log — and not a sample of the books. Nothing on this page is drafted prose from the manuscript.

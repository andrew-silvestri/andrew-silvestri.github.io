Texas and Oklahoma gas customers still pay for Winter Storm Uri

Customers of ten Texas and Oklahoma gas utilities are paying off Winter Storm Uri's gas costs until as late as 2047. A home pays $4 to $8 a month, and over a third of the total is interest. In February 2021, during the storm, the price utilities paid for gas jumped; the Oklahoma Corporation Commission's order for one utility describes1 "increases of as much as 100 times the typical purchase price, or more". Passed on at once, the costs would have pushed monthly home bills "in excess of $1,000", the Commission found for Oklahoma Natural Gas2. Instead, under laws both states passed in 20213, the costs were financed with bonds, which spread them, with interest, over many years of bills rather than one winter's. Customers of eight Texas utilities and of Oklahoma Natural Gas and Summit Utilities in Oklahoma repay them through a charge on their gas bills. $4.80 billion went to the utilities; customers repay $7.53 billion, $2.67 billion of it interest. So far $1.37 billion has been paid; $6.17 billion is still to come.

The bond schedules are fixed. Filings reset the charges at least once a year. This page shows the charges filed through October 2026 and does not update itself. What is still to come is counted from October 24, 2026.

  1. In Texas, the law sets one charge on each unit of gas for all customers who buy gas from the utilities in the program. The charge is now $1.13 per thousand cubic feet on most of their tariffs, about $4 a month for an average home. It has been billed since October 2023, and it continues until the bonds are repaid, in April 2039.
  2. In Oklahoma, the charge is a flat amount on each monthly bill, billed since late 2022. An Oklahoma Natural Gas home now pays $4.87 or $7.84, until 2047. The amount depends on whether the home uses less or more than 50 dekatherms of gas a year. A Summit home now pays $5.84, until 2037.
  3. A home on the system for the whole term pays about $750 in Texas and $970 to $2,440 in Oklahoma. Interest is about a third of that, and more for an Oklahoma Natural Gas home.

What remains to be repaid

Three bond issues carry the costs4: one in Texas, for eight utilities, and one for each of the two Oklahoma gas utilities that took part. Each issue has a fixed schedule of payments, set when it was sold. The chart adds the three schedules together. It shows what customers' charges must still pay, month by month, as scheduled.

$7.5 billion to repay. Oklahoma's two gas bond issues were sold in August 2022 and October 2022, and Texas's issue in March 2023. From then on, customers' charges must cover $7.53 billion. The shaded gap between the lines is the interest still to pay, $2.6 billion. With the $79.4 million that Texas paid at the start, the interest is $2.7 billion in total.

October 2026: $6.17 billion to go. $1.37 billion has been paid. $1.8 billion of the interest, which is most of it, is still to be paid. The principal decreases slowly at first, so interest continues to accumulate on a large balance for years.

April 2039: Texas's last payment. Summit's schedule ends earlier, in October 2037. The amount left after Texas, $0.77 billion, is for the Oklahoma Natural Gas bonds.

May 2047: the final payment. The Oklahoma Natural Gas bonds have the longest term, about twenty-five years from their sale.

Scheduled, not measured: the line shows the bonds' own payment schedules, less what Texas paid at the start from the bond proceeds (see Method). Where the money goes. $4.80 billion repays the costs that the commissions approved. $2.67 billion is interest. The remainder, $59.8 million, paid for the sale of the bonds and for the reserves that the proceeds funded.

In Texas, the Railroad Commission approved $3.38 billion for eight gas utilities. The bond document names them as follows: Atmos Energy $2.02 billion, CenterPoint $1.10 billion, Texas Gas Service $197.4 million, UniGas (Universal Natural Gas) $32.4 million, SiEnergy $18.8 million, EPCOR $11.3 million, Bluebonnet $2.0 million and Corix $0.3 million. Part of CenterPoint's share belongs to Summit Utilities, which bought some of CenterPoint's Texas system after the costs were set. Summit collects the charge there, and Summit's Oklahoma business, below, was also CenterPoint's. In Oklahoma, the Corporation Commission approved Oklahoma Natural Gas's gas costs "incurred beginning February 11, 2021, and ending February 19, 2021", $1,284.1 million of them. It also approved Summit's gas costs "incurred beginning February 7, 2021, and ending February 21, 2021", $75.7 million. Summit filed these costs when it was still CenterPoint Energy's Oklahoma business. Carrying costs and other approved costs are added to each. The Texas issuer's official statement, written to sell the bonds, gives the background in its own words: "In February 2021, prolonged frigid temperatures and winter precipitation, along with other weather-related effects of Winter Storm Uri, resulted in a dramatic reduction of natural gas availability and a concurrent surge in energy demand. As a result, gas utilities were forced to pay extraordinarily high prices for natural gas in order to meet demand."

As the Texas bonds were being sold, two bills were filed in the Texas Legislature: Senate Bill 30 on March 2, 2023 and House Bill 500 on March 7. Each proposed that the state pay the charges on customers' behalf. Customers pay them still: the charge has been on bills since October 2023, and it is in the tariffs filed for October 2026.

Who pays, and how

In Texas, the law sets one charge for all customers: "uniform monthly volumetric customer rate relief charges must be paid by all current and future customers" of the participating utilities. Future customers also pay it, whether or not they lived there in 2021. Only the utilities' sales customers pay the charge, per unit of gas, inside the gas-cost line of the bill. Customers who buy their gas from other suppliers and pay the utility only for delivery do not pay it. To make collections meet the payments, it is reset at least once a year7: $1.10 per thousand cubic feet from October 2023, $1.18 from October 2024, $1.20 from September 2025 and $1.13 from October 2026. One division's tariff on file is different: Atmos Energy's West Texas division shows $0.2140 per hundred cubic feet from June 2026, almost twice the others. Its filing does not say why, and this page has not found the reason.

In Oklahoma, the charge is a flat amount on each monthly bill, the same in a cold month as in a mild month. Each class of customer carries a share, which both of the Commission's orders set "based on actual usage during the month of February 2021". The shares can be reset, and Oklahoma Natural Gas's shares were reset. Its smaller-use homes had 13.84% of the total at issue and 13.15% in the March 2026 true-up. Both orders exclude customers on the utility's voluntary fixed-price plan in February 2021 and customers in the Low Income Heating Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP). Oklahoma Natural Gas divides homes by yearly use, under or over 50 dekatherms; the dekatherm measures the heat of the gas, not its volume. Oklahoma's average home used 62 thousand cubic feet a year over 2015 to 2024.

What it costs a home

Your utility:

Texas, the participating utilities: $1.13 per thousand cubic feet since October 2026 on most of their tariffs (the one exception is above). A home that uses 42 thousand cubic feet a year, Texas's average over 2015 to 2024, pays about $3.99 a month, averaged over the year. It pays more in a cold month and less in summer, and $48 a year in total. The bond document gave the first charge as about 5.8 per cent of an average home's bill at the large utilities. A home on the system from October 2023 until the bonds are repaid pays about $750 in total. This total uses the rates filed so far and today's rate after that. Interest is about 34 per cent of it.

Oklahoma Natural Gas, homes that use under 50 dekatherms a year: $4.87 on each monthly bill from May 2026, or $58 a year. The filed estimate from November 2026 is $4.60, and the charge was $6.33 when it began. Over the whole term, to 2047, the class's share of the repayment is about $1,540 for each home. Interest is about 40 per cent of it.

Oklahoma Natural Gas, homes that use 50 dekatherms a year or more: $7.84 on each monthly bill from May 2026, or $94 a year. The filed estimate from November 2026 is $7.42, and the charge was $10.04 when it began. Over the whole term, to 2047, the class's share of the repayment is about $2,440 for each home. Interest is about 40 per cent of it.

Summit Utilities Oklahoma, homes: $5.84 on each monthly bill from May 2026 (the charge was $5.26 when it began), or $70 a year. Over the whole term, to 2037, the class's share of the repayment is about $970 for each home. Interest is about 32 per cent of it.

Totals over the term are for a home on the system for all of the term. They are approximations, because the charges are reset at least once a year. Interest is a larger part of the early payments, when more is owed. It is a smaller part of each payment from now on. It is 27 per cent of what is still to come in Texas and 35 per cent in Oklahoma.

Method

Payments. Texas: the official statement's expected amortization schedule by tranche (A-1 $1,814.5 million at 5.102%, A-2 $1,707.3 million at 5.169%). This page computes the interest on the scheduled balance from the closing date, March 23, 2023, on the bonds' 30/360 basis. The first interest payment was on September 1, 2023, and the next payments are on each April 1 and October 1. The check: this method gives weighted average lives of 5.958 and 13.424 years, which are the lives the statement prints. Also, the balance at December 31, 2025 equals the issuer's report to the dollar. Oklahoma: the Issuance Advice Letters9' expected sinking fund schedules, row by row. This page reproduces every interest figure in them from the coupon and the balance to under a cent. What customers repay in Texas: the principal less the reserve, plus the interest, less the interest paid from the proceeds. In Texas, an account funded out of the proceeds paid the first interest payment, $79.4 million on September 1, 2023, before any charge was billed. A reserve of $17.6 million, also held back from the proceeds, pays the last instalment. Customers repay both through the principal, so this page does not count them again. The account held six months of interest, about $90.4 million at the coupons, against the $79.4 million that the first payment needed. The statement says that unused up-front funds reduce the first reset of the charge. Thus the Texas total may be up to about $11 million lower. What customers repay in Oklahoma: the principal plus the interest. The reserve funds, $7.2 million together, are not netted, because the documents opened for this page do not say how they are used. The reserve funds would change the total by 0.3 per cent. Ongoing costs (trustee, servicer, the issuers' expenses) are about $2.5 million a year across the three issues. The same charges pay them, but they are not in the totals above.

A home. Texas: the filed rate in each year from October, then today's rate to the last bill, multiplied by EIA's residential consumption per residential consumer10. That consumption is the average over 2015 to 2024 (42 thousand cubic feet a year; 2024 alone was 37.1). The charge is stated at a pressure base of 14.65 pounds per square inch. EIA counts gas at 60 degrees Fahrenheit and 14.73 pounds standard per square inch. Thus this page multiplies the rate by 14.73/14.65, which gives $1.1362 per thousand cubic feet. That is the rate CenterPoint's tariff states at 14.73 ($0.11362 per hundred). Texas Gas Service says its average home11 pays about $3.96 a month at $1.13. Oklahoma: the filings' own mechanism. This page divides a class's share of the scheduled payments and the stated ongoing costs by the class's customers when the charge was set. The page gives that figure because the charges are set in that way. Today's charge multiplied by the months of the term gives different totals: $1,440, $2,320 and $1,040. They are different because the charge has been both higher and lower than today's. A home's interest share includes the ongoing costs in the amount repaid.

Limits

This page adds up the amounts that the filings fix. It does not follow the payments to the sellers of the gas. It does not compare bonds with other ways the costs could have been recovered; the commissions' orders and staff reports make that comparison. It covers only the gas utilities' bond issues. It excludes gas customers of Texas utilities that did not take part. It also excludes customers who buy their gas from other suppliers and have it delivered (Texas's charge is on sales customers). It does not include the parts of Texas Gas Service's area that its tariff excludes. It does not include Oklahoma utilities whose rates the Corporation Commission does not set, such as city-owned systems, which a separate law covers12. The totals include homes only, not the other classes. Payments are as scheduled; collections can be ahead or behind, and the true-ups correct the difference.

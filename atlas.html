The atlas

Seven layers of one model. 109,296 nodes and 74,155 weighted links.

Open the atlas full screen

The atlas runs entirely in the browser. Choose a scenario and press Run. The side panel reports how many nodes changed, how many steps the model needed to settle, and the largest single effect. The counts on the tabs show where the change landed in the other layers. Click any node to read its data source and apply a change there.

Why a globe, and why a brain

Six of the seven layers are drawn on a sphere rather than a flat map. Mercator inflates Greenland to the size of Africa and stretches the high-latitude Russian and Canadian grids into ribbons. A good deal of this model's fuel supply lives at those latitudes. On a globe every node sits at its true angular position, and a point on the far side is hidden by the planet instead of being flung to the edge of a rectangle. The graticule runs every thirty degrees so that rotation reads as rotation.

The seventh layer is not a place, so it has no business on a map. Behaviour is drawn inside a brain, with the six channels at anatomically defensible positions: present bias in the prefrontal cortex, dissonance at the anterior cingulate near the midline, the norm channel at the temporo-parietal junction, status in the ventral striatum, habit in the basal ganglia, loss aversion in the amygdala. The solid is generated rather than traced, an ellipsoid given a longitudinal fissure and a few folds, with a cerebellum and a stem attached. It reads as a brain at a glance and it is not anatomy. The application says so on screen. The region names, and which channel sits in which, are real.

Anatomy and behaviour

The behaviour layer holds 24 channels, and that number is not going up. It is roughly the inventory of distinct, named mechanisms from behavioural economics and decision neuroscience that bear on how energy gets used. Filling a brain with two hundred of them would mean inventing mechanisms to occupy anatomy.

So the anatomy is drawn separately. 840 named structures of the Allen Mouse Brain Common Coordinate Framework sit inside the shell at the centre of mass the atlas publishes for each one, rescaled but not otherwise moved. They carry no weight, no edge and no channel, and they are held outside the node arrays entirely, so nothing can mistake scenery for part of the model. The 24 channels that do carry weight sit among them and wire into demand.

Reading a dense scene

Twenty-four thousand points is more than anyone can take in at once, so the sidebar carries a threshold that hides everything below a chosen weight. Weight is generating capacity for a power plant and resilience for everything else, which is the quantity the propagation engine already uses. At thirty per cent the scene falls to about fifteen thousand nodes, at sixty to under seven hundred, and at eighty-five to the few dozen largest objects in the system.

Hidden nodes still take part in the run. The threshold changes the view, not the answer.
